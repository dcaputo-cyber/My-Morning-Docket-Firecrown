[DEEP DIVE]

Today's deep dive is the UK Information Commission and what the Data Use and Access Act means for a media holding company with UK-facing properties.

Let's start with the institutional change, because it matters as a signal. 

Under Statutory Instrument 2026/1015, in force September thirtieth, 2026, the ICO was abolished as an office and its functions moved to the Information Commission.

 The rebrand is not merely cosmetic. 

The Data Use and Access Act's main provisions commenced on February fifth, 2026, the complaints requirement on June nineteenth, 2026, and the ICO's governance reforms on September thirtieth, 2026.

 What that sequencing means in practice is that the enforcement regime is now fully operational. The regulator has new powers, new procedures, and a new institutional identity, all of which signal an intent to use them.

What are the new enforcement powers specifically? 

The DUAA introduces a new power allowing the Information Commission to compel a person to attend an interview and answer questions if they work, or have worked, for or on behalf of an organization subject to data protection law, and this applies if the Commission suspects that a person or organization has failed to comply.

 That is a materially different investigatory posture from what existed before. The old ICO could request information; the new Commission can compel testimony. 

The Commission had also consulted in late 2025 on new enforcement procedural guidance introducing a structured settlement procedure.

 A structured settlement framework means the regulator has a pathway to resolve matters that stops short of a full public enforcement decision, which may actually accelerate case resolution volume, since the barrier to opening and closing a matter is lower.

What changed in substantive data protection terms? The most practically relevant change for a media company is the cookie consent question. 

As of November 2026, the cookie consent reforms under the DUAA have not yet taken effect, because no commencement instrument for the new Regulation 6A had been laid.

 So your UK properties are still operating under the pre-DUAA PECR cookie framework. The transition to a less burdensome consent regime is coming, but it has not arrived. Do not let your compliance team declare victory on cookies yet.

The other substantive change that is in force is the recognised legitimate interests basis. 

The Act adds a new lawful basis for processing, described as recognised legitimate interests. Processing under this basis is lawful if it is necessary for one of the purposes on a fixed list, with no balancing test. The list covers public interest purposes: disclosures to public bodies, national security and defence, emergencies, crime, and safeguarding. Ordinary commercial data collection is not on it.

 This is a narrower carve-out than many in the ad tech industry hoped for. It does not create a broad commercial legitimate interest basis that could replace consent for behavioral advertising. The consent obligation for UK users running behavioral ad campaigns remains intact.

The enforcement track record going into this new era is worth noting. 

The Information Commission has launched investigations into how social media and video-sharing platforms use UK children's personal information, with the Reddit and Imgur enforcement actions in 2026 marking the beginning of heightened enforcement in this area.

 Children's data is a priority. 

The fine ceiling remains seventeen point five million pounds or four percent of global annual turnover, whichever is higher, as demonstrated when the ICO fined Imgur's owner MediaLab two hundred forty-seven thousand five hundred ninety pounds over children's data earlier in 2026.



For Firecrown specifically, here are three concrete actions. First, map your UK user data flows against the DUAA's new processing bases now. The structured settlement procedure means enforcement can move faster than it did under the old regime, and an internal audit before a complaint arrives is far cheaper than one conducted under compulsion. Second, do not stand down on UK cookie compliance under the assumption that the reform is coming. It is not yet in force. Your consent management platform for UK users should still be meeting the pre-DUAA PECR standard. Third, if any of your brands have UK users who are or could be under the age of eighteen, specifically FLYING or any motorsports-adjacent Firecrown properties, treat children's data as the enforcement priority it demonstrably is. The Reddit and Imgur actions this year signal that the Information Commission is specifically targeting platforms where children may be present.
